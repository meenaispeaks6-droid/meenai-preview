Why your website should pay for itself in 90 days

A website is not a brochure. It is a salesperson who never sleeps, and it should be measured like one.

Most businesses buy a website the way they buy furniture: pick something that looks right, pay once, and stop thinking about it. Then they wonder why it never produces anything.

The useful question is not "how much does a website cost?" but "what would a salesperson who works 24/7, never takes leave and speaks to every visitor personally be worth to you?"

Measure it like a hire

A website that earns its keep does three things measurably: it attracts the right visitors, it converts them into enquiries, and it qualifies those enquiries before they reach a human. If you cannot name the number of enquiries it produced last month, it is decoration.

What changes when you measure

  • Copy gets written for the buyer's decision, not the founder's taste
  • Speed stops being a technical detail and becomes a revenue line
  • Every form gets a follow-up, and every follow-up gets tracked

Ninety days is enough time to launch, learn and tune. If the site has not paid for itself by then, the problem is usually scope: it was built to impress, not to sell.
